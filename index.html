The problem

Structure elucidation shouldn’t be a bottleneck.

Expert time is scarce

Assigning signals by hand takes years of experience and hours of attention for every compound.

Tools are scattered

Predictors, sketchers and 3D viewers live in separate programs, each with its own licence and installation.

Compute is expensive

Running ML models or simulations takes hardware and administration that most labs simply don’t have.

NMR is hard to learn

Students learn to read spectra from static textbook figures instead of working with real, interactive data.

How it works

One workflow, from raw data to a verified structure.

01

Upload & clean the spectrum

Drop a JCAMP-DX file, including Bruker variants. Set the spectrometer frequency, tune the baseline, mark solvent peaks and let automatic peak detection do the rest.

02

Machine learning algorithms propose candidate structures

Our models read the peak list and propose candidate structures. Each one is built from chemical groups you can inspect, so you never get a black-box answer.

03

Refine it your way

Edit at the level of chemical groups or individual atoms. The two views stay in sync, and a library of 374 named groups labels every piece of the molecule.

04

Verify against the experiment

Predict the ¹³C spectrum of your candidate and overlay it on the measured one. Compare candidates side by side and keep the structure that fits.

The workspace

Built around the way chemists think.

Groups, atoms, spectra and 3D in one place. Every edit is reflected everywhere at once.

Atoms and groups, in sync

Draw or edit atoms and bonds directly, and the molecule is decomposed back into named chemical groups automatically.

374 named chemical groups

Search by name, alias or SMILES, or browse by category, from methyl to morpholinyl.

Start from a name

Type “paracet…”, pick paracetamol and it lands on the canvas. You can also paste SMILES or InChI. The lookup runs on our own index, so your queries never leave the platform.

Interactive 3D

Rotate and inspect every candidate structure in 3D, right next to its spectrum.

Collaboration

Work on the same structure, at the same time.

Share a workspace the way you share a spreadsheet. Everyone sees the same spectrum and the same candidate, and every edit appears live.

  • Live presence. See who is in the workspace and where they are working.
  • Supervisor and student on one spectrum, instead of screenshots sent by email.
  • CRO teams reviewing a structure together, and with their client.

Learning mode

Learn NMR by doing, not by memorising.

Guided exercises on real spectra teach you to read shifts, recognise groups and reason your way to a structure, with feedback at every step.

  • Students: practise on the same tools researchers use.
  • Educators: run hands-on classes without installing anything.
  • Anyone curious: learn chemistry and NMR at your own pace.
